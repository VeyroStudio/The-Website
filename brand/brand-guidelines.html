Brand guidelines

How the VEYRO identity is built, and the rules that keep it consistent wherever it turns up.

Version 2.0 — Internal document

01 — The brand

What VEYRO is

VEYRO builds websites for local businesses — barbers, salons, takeaways, butchers, cafés — around Wideopen, Gosforth, Killingworth and the wider North East, and remotely for anyone in the UK.

The people we sell to have usually been quoted £2,000 by a “proper” agency and decided websites aren’t for people like them. So the single most important job this brand has is to not look expensive or intimidating. Warm, plain, competent, local. It should feel like a good tradesperson, not a design consultancy.

Warm

Cream, not white. Rounded, not sharp. Approachable before it is impressive.

Plain

Prices on the page. No jargon. Nothing that needs decoding.

Local

Town names, a real phone number, a real person. Proximity is the advantage — say it everywhere.

The failure mode for this brand is looking too good. If a design decision makes VEYRO feel like a London agency, it is the wrong decision — however nice it looks.

02 — Logo

The mark

An asymmetric V. The right arm overshoots the point where a V would normally stop and keeps climbing, so it reads as a letterform and an upward direction at once. The final segment of that arm carries the amber — the only place the accent appears in the identity by default.

Both the mark and the wordmark are drawn as vector paths, not set in a typeface. Nothing to license, nothing to re-outline, identical at every size on every device.

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space on all four sides equal to the full height of the mark. Nothing enters it — no text, no rules, no image edges, no other logos.

Misuse

Do

  • Use the supplied SVGs, unmodified
  • Place on flat cream, white or navy
  • Switch to mono when only one colour is available
  • Scale proportionally, from the corner

Don't

  • Recolour the mark or move the amber accent
  • Rotate, skew, stretch, outline or add effects
  • Add a drop shadow, glow, gradient or container of your own
  • Re-set the wordmark in a font or change its letterspacing
  • Place it over a busy photo without a plain panel behind it
  • Rebuild it by hand — always use the source files

The one rule that matters

Amber is a background colour, not a text colour. At 2.74:1 on cream it fails accessibility for text. For amber-looking text and links use Amber ink (#8F5210), which passes at 5.81:1. Text sitting on an amber button is always Navy — cream on amber fails at 2.74:1.

CMYK values are a conversion starting point, not a print spec. Confirm with your printer and ask for a proof — amber shifts noticeably on uncoated stock. For rich black in print use C60 M40 Y40 K100 rather than the navy screen value.

04 — Typography

Two families

A characterful grotesque for headings and a quiet, highly legible sans for everything else. Both are open source and free for commercial use, with no licence to track.

Display — Bricolage Grotesque

Are you there?

Headings only, weight 600. Tracking −0.028em, leading 1.02. It has personality without being a novelty face — which is exactly the register this brand needs. Never below 18px, never for body copy.

Body & UI — Public Sans

The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. 0123456789 £99

All body copy, buttons, navigation, labels and form fields. Weights 400, 500 and 600. Body leading 1.65, measure capped at 66 characters. Minimum 16px on mobile — below that iOS zooms the viewport whenever a field is focused.

Public Sans was drawn for US government services, which is the highest compliment available here: it is designed to be read by everyone, on anything, without being noticed.

The amber marker sweep behind a key word is a signature device. Use it once per page, on the single word the whole headline turns on. Two of them and it stops meaning anything.

05 — Motion

How things move

Motion here is quiet and single-axis. Things arrive from below and settle. Nothing bounces, nothing spins except a loading indicator, and nothing moves that the reader did not cause.

DeviceBehaviour
Section reveal850ms, opacity 0→1 with a 24px rise, as it enters view
Headline reveal1050ms per line, masked, sliding up with an 85ms stagger
Marker sweepAmber grows left-to-right behind the key word, 1.1s, delayed 350ms so the line lands first
Search demoTypes the query, drops in three competitors, then reveals “you” below. Plays once.
List staggerTick items in 60ms increments, eight steps maximum
Card lift4px rise, soft shadow, amber border on hover
Scroll progressAmber bar across the top, native CSS scroll timeline
Sticky mobile barSlides up past 600px scroll — Call and Get a quote
Easingcubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) for entrances — fast out, long settle
Animated propertiestransform and opacity only — never width, height, top or left

Do

  • Animate one or two things per screen
  • Reveal each element once, then leave it alone
  • Honour prefers-reduced-motion everywhere
  • Follow the reading order — top to bottom

Don't

  • Hijack the scroll or fight the reader's input
  • Loop anything decorative
  • Shift layout on hover
  • Put content behind an animation someone has to wait out

Motion sensitivity is a genuine accessibility need, not a preference to design around. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce every animation resolves instantly to its finished state — nothing is hidden and nothing is lost.

06 — Voice

How VEYRO writes

Like a capable person explaining something over a counter. Short sentences, concrete claims, British spelling, first person singular — it is one person, and pretending otherwise is the fastest way to lose the trust the whole business rests on.

Sounds like VEYRO

  • “Your customers are Googling you. Are you there?”
  • “It's about the price of two haircuts a month.”
  • “You do nothing. I build it, I run it, you carry on cutting hair.”
  • “You'll always be told the number before you agree to anything.”

Doesn't

  • “We leverage bespoke digital solutions.”
  • “Our team of experts…” (there is no team)
  • “Award-winning, industry-leading, world-class.”
  • “Take your brand to the next level! 🚀”
ConventionStandard
PersonFirst person singular — I, me, my. Never “we”.
SpellingBritish — organise, colour, enquiry, licence (noun)
Studio nameVEYRO in full capitals, always
PricesAlways shown. £99, £199, £299 — no “from”, no “POA”
DashesEm dash with spaces — like this
EmojiNever on the website. Fine in a Facebook DM.
Exclamation marksEffectively never
ClaimsOnly what can be honoured. No client counts, no invented testimonials, until they are real.

07 — Accessibility

Non-negotiable

A lot of the people buying from VEYRO are not confident with technology, and a fair number will be reading on an old phone with the text size turned up. This matters more here than it does for most brands.

RequirementStandard
Text contrast4.5:1 minimum for body, 3:1 for large text
Touch targets44×44px minimum for anything tappable
Body text on mobile16px minimum, no exceptions
Focus statesVisible 2px amber-ink ring, 3px offset, on every interactive element
IconsSVG only. Icon-only buttons carry an aria-label.
ColourNever the only way information is conveyed
AnimationAnything that conveys meaning also has a text equivalent
Reduced motionFully honoured — see section 05
TargetWCAG 2.2 AA across all pages
